The Simple Present Golden Rule

Summaries and literary analyses must always be written in Simple Present.

He / She / It $\rightarrow$ Das -s muss mit!

  • The author describes (not describe)
  • Julian tries (try $\rightarrow$ tries)
  • The text shows (not show)

Past Tense Trap Words

Avoid these common past tense forms in summaries:

was / were $\rightarrow$ is / are
had $\rightarrow$ has / have
went $\rightarrow$ goes
thought $\rightarrow$ thinks
Adjective

Describes a Noun

Answers: What kind? How is it?

e.g. cold wind, steep ridge, helpless girl

Adverb

Modifies a Verb / Adjective

Answers: How? In what manner? (Often ends in -ly)

e.g. walks slowly, extremely dangerous

Noun

Person, Place, Thing, Idea

Answers: Who? What?

e.g. forest, fear, protagonist

Simile (Vergleich)

Direct comparison using "like" or "as".

"whipping ice crystals like sharp needles"

Metaphor (Metapher)

Indirect comparison without using "like/as".

"a sea of thick, grey fog"

Personification (Personifikation)

Giving human qualities to non-human things.

"towering trees that seemed to whisper"

Alliteration (Alliteration)

Repetition of initial consonant sounds.

"feeling freezing fears"
